Literature inputs and scope of the Lean development

This is relative formal verification of the Jellium–UEG equality. Charges,
finite-energy states, Sobolev spaces and their observables are explicit
interfaces. Physical trace-class Fock operators are not constructed from Lean
foundations. Every literature or operator law is a visible theorem hypothesis.
No law asserts either desired comparison or the new canonical completion bound.

General results in the manuscript's Basic facts section are used through the
existing explicit law interfaces. Appendix A now sketches the limit on balls.
Its elementary sequence-convergence lemma is proved in Lean, but the full
thermodynamic limit and its operator-theoretic construction remain inputs.

The same ChargeModel and StateAPI are used on both sides. The lower confined
predicate and upper near-ground-ball interface are separately supplied laws
with the same scalar eJ. Their identification with the same concrete canonical
Dirichlet model is part of the physical interpretation of those inputs, not
a constructed equivalence of concrete operator domains.
Density functions may
be chosen representatives, while constrained search and Coulomb identities
respect almost-everywhere equality. The physical Coulomb form includes 1/2.
The reference density and kinetic coefficient are positive, and the finite
spin multiplicity is the same throughout.

1. Canonical Dirichlet Jellium thermodynamic limit and stability

Primary source
E. H. Lieb and H. Narnhofer, The Thermodynamic Limit for Jellium,
Journal of Statistical Physics 12 (1975), 291–310.
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01012066
https://fig.if.usp.br/~marchett/rg/thermodynamic-limit-jellium_lieb-narnhofer.pdf

LowerPhysical.StandardLaws.ln is the canonical ground-energy limit restricted
to neutral balls. Its energy is the actual constrained infimum over confined
fixed-number states. Section 5.4(iii) includes the ground-state limit. No
identification with unconfined grand-canonical Jellium is taken as an input.
The former disconnected-domain LN specialization is not used in the new proof.

Neutralization.StandardLaws.onsager retains the positive smeared Coulomb term
from the ordinary Onsager inequality. A signed charge of mass Q supported in
B_(R+a) has Coulomb energy at least Q^2/(2(R+a)). This follows by subtracting Q
times the uniform probability charge on the sphere and using positivity.
The resulting finite-sector lower bound is a general electrostatic input.
Lean derives the required charge bound from it rather than assuming that
low-energy sectors have surface-order charge. The coefficient is now
2*(1+a)*c + sqrt(2*(1+a)*(C+c*b)), with b = 4*pi*rho/3. For unit smearing it
is 4*c + 2*sqrt(C+c*b), as in the revised appendix. The annular geometry
also proves the appendix's 8*pi*R^2 lower bound on the trimmed volume.

The local shell operations adapt the sampling/penalty/disjoint-orbital method
in LN Section 5.1. The published leftover-region lemma is not invoked as a
ready-made completion theorem. Appendix B of the manuscript writes out the
adaptation, retaining the core–shell interaction.

Neutralization.StandardLaws.sample now constructs an OrbitalSample. Its law is
the finite product of normalized Lebesgue measure on the trimmed annulus.
OrbitalSampling proves the exact classical Coulomb expectation, including the
negative omitted diagonal term, and almost-sure distinctness of the centers.
OrbitalGeometry assigns half the minimum of one and the nearest-neighbour
distance as each orbital radius. It proves disjointness, confinement and the
kinetic bound by a short-distance inverse-square pair penalty.
OrbitalRadial defines the sine reduced profile and the associated Euclidean
orbital function. It proves reduced mass one and Dirichlet integral pi^2/r^2.
The radial Newton kernel then gives the point-charge potential outside the
ball and bounds the background-smearing loss by pi*rho/2 when r <= 1/2.
OrbitalKernel proves the penalty's three-dimensional integrability and exact
integral 32*pi^3. These results give the numerical constant
kineticCoeff*(4*pi^2 + 16*pi^3) + (pi/2)*abs(rho) used by the sampler.

OrbitalConstruction.OperatorLaws retains the general Sobolev/Fock product
law for arbitrary smooth normalized reduced profiles with zero endpoint
values. This law supplies a confined many-body state and its exact energy
decomposition. The identification of those profiles with physical Sobolev
orbitals and the construction of antisymmetrized Fock states are not rebuilt
here. The exact pair Newton identity, radial averaging of the core potential,
and the polar/Fubini background identity also remain explicit inputs. The
background identity uses a measurable angular occupation fraction between
zero and one. Lean derives its loss bound rather than assuming one.
StandardLaws.shell_pairing is an exact Coulomb expansion for an arbitrary
real uniform annular charge and a confined core. It does not concern a sampled
configuration or assert an energy estimate. Integrability of the core's
one-body potential on the sampling annulus remains an analytic input.

Lean proves the local orbital energy bound, favorable-sample selection,
integer completion, charge cancellation and surface-order bound from these
general laws. The strengthened
exists_neutral_completion_with_monopole retains the favorable negative
charge-square term at the outer radius before the original completion theorem
drops it. Selection is at or below the exact mean and retains the omitted
self-interaction correction, with no added unit error. The completion uses
the outer-radius shell estimate with the excess-background sign convention
of the manuscript. No remaining law assumes the orbital cost, iid penalty
estimate, total neutrality, bounded charge imbalance or surface-order
completion energy. Those conclusions are derived. The physical Fock-space
implementation remains outside the development.

The upper construction's NearGroundBallLaw is the canonical ball limit followed
by the variational principle and rotation averaging. It concerns the same
canonical Jellium energy as the lower comparison. The current manuscript
chooses rotation-invariant exact ground states. Lean retains the sufficient
near-ground-state bounds and does not add an attainment assumption. Likewise,
the lower proof uses constrained-search near-minimizers instead of the exact
minimizers now chosen in the manuscript.

2. Fixed-density UEG and localization

Primary source
M. Lewin, E. H. Lieb and R. Seiringer, The Local Density Approximation in
Density Functional Theory, Pure and Applied Analysis 2 (2020), 35–73.
https://doi.org/10.2140/paa.2020.2.35
https://msp.org/paa/2020/2-1/paa-v2-n1-p03-p.pdf
https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04046

Theorem 1 supplies the fixed-smoothed-density UEG limit. Its mollifier has unit
mass, compact support and finite square-root Dirichlet integral. The smoothing
scale satisfies delta/R -> 0 and delta*R -> infinity. LowerPhysical's ueg field
is its specialization to balls. InnerDensity proves both conditions for the
actual new density sequence, its strict support margin and the discrepancy
estimate. None of those construction-specific conclusions is an input.

Published Proposition 16, equation (47), supplies the averaged lower
localization inequality. It is Proposition 2 in arXiv v2. Theorem 1 has the
same numbering in both versions. At fixed smoothing thickness the localization
error is O(1/l) times the integral of density plus density squared.
UpperDensity and Boundary derive the needed bounded-transition-layer result
from this standard inequality and the large-cell UEG limit.

Earlier primary UEG source
M. Lewin, E. H. Lieb and R. Seiringer, Statistical mechanics of the uniform
electron gas, Journal de l'Ecole polytechnique — Mathematiques 5 (2018), 79–116.
https://jep.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jep.64/

Section 5 provides the quantum grand-canonical UEG framework. Its whole-space
Jellium relaxation is not assumed to have the same limit as canonical LN
Jellium. The stronger fixed-convolution limit used here comes from the 2020
paper.

Underlying localization source
G. M. Graf and D. Schenker, On the molecular limit of Coulomb gases,
Communications in Mathematical Physics 174 (1995), 215–227.
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099471

Its smeared inequality is the source used by the LLS localization argument.
This is Graf–Schenker localization, not the Graf–Solovej correlation estimate.

StateAPI supplies ordinary quasifree and disjoint-support Fock product laws.
Its quasifree input is a bounded-density estimate with a gradient term and
M^(2/3) times the mass. This is sufficient for the constructed fluid, whose
density has a proved uniform bound. It does not formalize the full epsilon
dependent LLS fluid lemma for arbitrary unbounded densities or its displayed
numerical constants. The measurable choice of such a fluid state remains
part of FockOperations.
MeasurableTrial and Variational supply translation, measurable finite products
and continuum mixtures, including the required integrability. The upper
construction proves that these conditions hold for its actual trial family.
The indirect-energy functional is never assumed affine in the state.

LowerPhysical.SectorData is the ordinary number-sector decomposition, with a
summable energy expectation and admissible occupied sectors. There is no
prescribed mean particle number and no sector rotation. exists_sector_le proves
the selection of a sector no higher in energy than the average.

4. General Coulomb, Sobolev and elementary analytic laws

The ChargeModel uses a real vector space of finite charges embedded into
functions. Background evaluation is required for bounded measurable domains.
Coulomb symmetry, bilinearity, positivity and potential pairing are inputs.
Standard Newton and polar integral formulas identify physical three-dimensional
radial charges with RadialShells' one-dimensional mass representation.
NeutralizationElectrostatics proves the new shell cancellation and estimates
using that representation.

The Coulomb interpolation bound
D(f) <= C*||f||_1^(5/3)*||f||_infinity^(1/3)
is the usual near/far kernel estimate. InnerDensity proves the exact missing
mass, its polynomial surface bound and the resulting vanishing normalized
Coulomb energy. The former BV and density-contraction inputs are unnecessary.

M. Hoffmann-Ostenhof and T. Hoffmann-Ostenhof, Schrodinger inequalities and
asymptotic behavior of the electron density of atoms and molecules,
Physical Review A 16 (1977), 1782–1785.
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.16.1782

The square-root density inequality, ordinary Sobolev chain rules, polar
integration and convolution regularity are standard analytic inputs.
SmoothCollar, RadialCollar, SmoothCube and UpperBackground prove the needed
profile bounds, compensated collar costs and box-background properties.
Vitali approximation is the geometric input for the upper packing, whose
integer radii, separation, finite selection and limiting coverage are proved.

5. Derived conclusions and audit scope

ThermodynamicSequence formalizes the elementary scalar convergence lemma
lem:LN_sequences newly included in Appendix A. Its finite-sum envelope and
successor identity are proved directly, and bounded monotone convergence
gives the limit. This theorem is audited but is supplementary to the main
equality. The physical packing recursion and its quantum-radius rescaling
are not constructed here, and the canonical LN limit remains an input.

The active proof derives inner-density mass/support/scale estimates, its
Coulomb error, low-sector selection, the charge estimate, rounded annular
geometry, shell mass and radial cancellation, favorable orbital realization,
finite-volume canonical completion and the lower thermodynamic comparison.
The orbital realization uses the explicit profiles and proved estimates
through the general Fock product law described above.
The upper packing, collars, averaged density and upper comparison are proved
by their corresponding modules. The current proof also records the sharper
negative-part energy estimate and a maximum-radius transition width.
The packing-independent averaged density cap is proved separately. The
localization step uses the sufficient fixed-packing cap, since the packing
is held fixed throughout the large-box limit.
The upper thermodynamic limit uses exact averaged lattice coverage.
UniformCounting additionally proves a translation-uniform O(R^2) counting
error for the actual selected balls by comparing shifts and averaging exact
counts in enlarged and reduced cubes. Its constant may depend on the largest
radius as well as the period and collar width. UpperBox derives the explicit
uniform O(1/R) normalized energy remainder and the epsilon form of the
manuscript's fixed-packing supremum/limsup conclusion. These supplementary
results are not needed by the existing averaged trial-state proof.

EnergyScaling proves the algebraic reduction from hbar = 1 at all positive
densities and the equivalence with the lambda formulation, given the two
physical scaling relations. Main.equality and Main.coupling_equality now
connect those reductions to Conjecture51.equality through UnitParameterInputs
at every positive density. The density scaling law itself gives the parameter
scaling powers. The fixed finite spin multiplicity is encoded by the selected
state model and its constants. Concrete physical operator families as spin
or hbar varies are not constructed.

No premise asserts a GC limit, equality of ensembles, completion inequality,
new trial convergence, bounded-layer conclusion or either final comparison.
The old assignment and contraction arguments remain in historical helper
modules but are absent from the final theorem's dependency graph. Audit.lean
checks that exclusion, checks logical axioms, and prints the final theorem
and the explicit local shell input interfaces. Logical axiom checks do not
prove the physical interpretation of those interfaces.
